    Design Ideation

    Intro/overview

    To start off, our team initiated the ideation process by setting up a shared Google Slides presentation so that each member could add their ideas to the project. During the process, communication mostly happened on Discord, where the team members were able to talk about their ideas and coordinate their individual work. The first step was to identify the design requirements that were frequently mentioned during our research into existing garment-folding designs. These requirements then formed the basis for brainstorming various features and mechanisms that could be included in our own design.

    Once the design requirements had been established, the team set up an open table in the shared presentation so that the members could suggest various possible solutions. One design requirement was considered at a time, and a number of different devices, mechanisms, sensors, and other features that could be used to meet it were brainstormed. At this stage, the emphasis was on coming up with a wide range of ideas rather than right away deciding which of these ideas would be included in the final product. This method is in keeping with the aim of the assignment, since it stresses the generation of many individual product features that can then be combined to produce complete solutions.

    After the initial brainstorming session had been finished, we started to group the features that we thought would work well in combination. For instance, various combinations of actuators, sensors, safety devices, folding mechanisms, and user-interface features could be put together in order to arrive at different methods of achieving the same overall garment-folding process. This enabled us to progress from separate feature ideas to full product concepts. Instead of assessing each feature on its own, the team looked at how well the different components would complement one another.

    The team members then took time on their own to develop a full design by combining the features that had been generated earlier in the group brainstorming session. This allowed each of them to tackle the problem independently and as a result led to the production of ideas which were different from one another. Visual representations of the concepts were created using AI-assisted image generation, on the basis of the descriptions given by the team. These visual representations served to convey the intended layout, features and the general way in which each proposed design operated.

    Once the individual concepts had been finished, the team came back together to compare the various designs. The features and concepts were mainly ranked according to their cost-effectiveness and the frequency with which similar methods appeared during our research into existing designs. Solutions that were frequently used were taken into account since their repeated application offered examples of how such approaches could be applied in a garment-folding system, and cost-effectiveness allowed the team to assess whether a solution was reasonable given the scope of the project. The team also looked at how the different components functioned together in each complete concept.

    In the end, the team looked at the various designs and chose three concepts which we thought offered clear and effective ways of addressing the design problem. Instead of picking three designs that were almost the same, we went for concepts that showed different combinations of the features that had been developed during the brainstorming phase. The three concepts thus chosen were included in the project and used to illustrate the various possible avenues for the automated garment-folding system. By following this procedure, the team was able to progress in a systematic manner from the initial broad brainstorming through grouping and ranking the features to the development of three full product concepts, as the ideation process required.
